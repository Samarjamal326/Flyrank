FlyRank ML Internship · ML-11 Capstone

CTR / Engagement Opportunity Scoring

Can a leakage-safe machine-learning ranker prioritize content items for search-engagement review better than a transparent rule?

Abstract

This study asks whether a leakage-safe model can prioritize content items for search-engagement review better than a transparent rule. Using February 2026 decision features and a March 2026 benchmark-relative CTR outcome from the FlyRank internship warehouse, the analysis trains an interpretable Logistic Regression and compares it with the Week-4 baseline on the same client-held-out test set. The model's observed Precision@10 was 0.700, compared with 0.800 for the baseline. These results are measured on one validation slice and should be interpreted as directional decision-support evidence rather than proof that an intervention causes higher CTR. The resulting playbook ranks pages for human review while explicitly avoiding autonomous publishing or causal refresh claims.

1. Introduction and problem statement

Content teams can have more pages worth investigating than they can manually review. The practical objective is therefore a ranking problem: place pages most likely to merit CTR/engagement review near the top of a finite review queue.

Research question: Can February search-performance signals rank March benchmark-relative CTR-risk pages better than the Week-4 transparent baseline on clients that were not used for training?

Primary decision metric: Precision@10, because the intended operational use is a small top-of-queue review list.

2. Data and architecture

The warehouse is treated as the source of truth. Daily performance is aggregated into a February decision window and a March outcome window. The modeling unit is one pseudonymized client × content item. The content dimension is used only for the descriptive content-age analysis.

3. Methodology

Features

feb_impressions, feb_clicks, feb_ctr, and impression-weighted feb_avg_position.

Proxy target

For each position tier, the training clients define a February median CTR benchmark. A content item is positive when March CTR falls below that frozen benchmark. This is a benchmark-relative risk proxy, not a direct intervention-success label.

Transparent baseline

max(training_tier_median_ctr − feb_ctr, 0) × log(1 + feb_impressions). The rule is deliberately transparent: a larger measured CTR gap and stronger volume increase priority.

Validation

An 80/20 split is performed by client_hash_id. The position-tier benchmark is learned from training clients only and then frozen for held-out clients. March outcomes and label-derived fields are excluded from model features.

9. Ranked action playbook

  1. Prioritize CTR review for high-scoring items with sufficient measured volume.
  2. Review if capacity allows for moderate-scoring items with adequate evidence.
  3. Monitor / do not prioritize weak or low-evidence items.

Human review rule: verify measured volume, position context, available page/query context, recent edits, seasonality, business importance, and measurement gaps before acting.

No-go decisions

  • Do not auto-publish content changes from the score.
  • Do not claim a refresh will cause a CTR increase.
  • Do not publish client names, domains, URLs, raw queries, titles, or restricted search text.
  • Do not treat pseudonymous IDs as predictive features.

10. Limitations and honest framing

What the evidence supports

  • The learned ranker can be compared with the transparent baseline under the stated held-out validation design.
  • Client-grouped validation prevents the same client from appearing in both train and test.
  • The resulting score can support a human review queue.

What the evidence does not support

  • No causal claim that refreshing content increases CTR.
  • No causal claim that content age causes performance changes.
  • No long-term or production generalization guarantee from one February → March window.
  • No claim that the benchmark-relative proxy is equivalent to intervention success.
Defensible claim: The model provides a leakage-safe ranking of pages for CTR/engagement review; the validation result indicates whether this ranking is more precise than the transparent baseline on the held-out clients used in this study.

11. Monitoring and future validation

A production-like implementation should monitor Precision@10 drift, feature distribution drift, target/base-rate drift, missingness changes, position-tier mix, and reviewer overrides. Before production use, the methodology should be repeated across additional time windows or repeated client-grouped splits.
